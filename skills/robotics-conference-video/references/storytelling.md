# Academic storytelling

The narrative is a sequence of research questions and supporting evidence. Do not turn paper-section order into an obligatory video outline.

## Shared principles

One segment answers a defined question. Establish conditions before interpreting outcomes. Use comparisons where they change an inference. Include relevant limitations, uncertainty, selection, and scope. Do not repeat a finding only to fill time.

## Supplementary

Make the evaluation inspectable: inputs, method observability, evaluation conditions, comparisons, decisive evidence, and boundaries. Preserve the context needed to interpret an event or result. Explanatory animation is useful only when it helps interpret supplied evidence.

## Presentation

Organize around research question → gap → method → evaluation → main findings → limitations → conclusion. Pace for comprehension. Focus one panel or result at a time instead of shrinking an entire dense figure.

## Teaser

Use minimum context, central method logic, strongest supported evidence, and a bounded takeaway. Omitting secondary results is acceptable; omitting a condition that changes the apparent claim is not.

## Claim-to-segment check

State the question, claim ID, evidence-bearing content, conditions, and what would be lost if the segment were removed. Aesthetic appeal alone does not justify a repeated result.

## Narration

Use concise spoken sentences and calibrated claims. Distinguish direct observation from interpretation and open questions. Avoid unsupported superlatives or treating one selected example as an aggregate result.

Neutral synthetic wording for fixture documentation:

- "Synthetic Test A checks the rendering pipeline."
- "The two generated patterns share the same display clock."
- "This example contains no measured research result."

These are synthetic descriptions, not placeholders for a user's study.

## Caption timing

Time captions against actual speech. Preserve useful clause punctuation, avoid arbitrary wrapping, and place text away from the evidence-bearing content. A caption change does not imply a speech cut. An incomplete or approximate timing alignment must be documented.
